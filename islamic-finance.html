Why Islamic Finance

Finance That Serves People, Not Just Profit

Islamic finance is not simply banking without interest. It is a comprehensive ethical framework that promotes fairness, transparency, and risk-sharing between lenders and borrowers. At MMIC, we believe finance should uplift communities, not burden them.

Our entire product range is reviewed and certified by our Sharia Supervisory Board — qualified Islamic scholars who ensure every transaction complies with the principles of Fiqh al-Muamalat (Islamic commercial jurisprudence).

No Riba (Interest)

All transactions are free from usurious interest, which is explicitly prohibited in Islam.

Risk Sharing

Profit and loss are shared equitably between MMIC and the client — a partnership, not a burden.

Asset-Backed

Every financing must be linked to a real, tangible asset or service — no speculation.

Ethical Investing

We do not finance haram industries. All funds go to productive, community-building activities.

Islamic Finance Modes

Our Sharia-Compliant Product Structures

Each product type is explained below — what it means, how it works, and how MMIC uses it.

مُرَابَحَة

Murabaha

Cost-Plus Sale

What it means: MMIC purchases an asset (goods, equipment, livestock) and sells it to the client at a pre-agreed marked-up price, payable in installments. The markup is MMIC's profit — not interest.

How MMIC uses it: Used in FursadMaal (motorcycle financing), E-Fursad (technology assets), SME stock financing, and livestock finance.

Example: MMIC buys a motorcycle for $800, sells it to Ahmed for $960 payable over 12 months — $80/month. No hidden fees.
مُشَارَكَة

Musharakah

Joint Partnership

What it means: MMIC and the client jointly invest in a business venture. Profits are shared according to a pre-agreed ratio. Losses are shared in proportion to capital contributed.

How MMIC uses it: Applied in SME finance and fisheries finance where both parties actively contribute capital and expertise.

Example: MMIC contributes $3,000 and the client contributes $1,000 to a grocery business. Profits split 60/40 in favor of client until MMIC's capital is repaid.
مُضَارَبَة

Mudarabah

Profit Sharing

What it means: MMIC provides capital (rab al-mal) while the client provides expertise and labor (mudarib). Profits are shared per a pre-agreed ratio. Losses fall on MMIC unless caused by client negligence.

How MMIC uses it: Used for skilled entrepreneurs and traders who have expertise but lack capital — particularly in SME and women finance programs.

Example: MMIC provides $2,000 to Fadumo's tailoring business. They agree on a 70/30 profit split until MMIC's capital is recovered.
سَلَم

Salam

Advance Payment

What it means: MMIC pays full price upfront for goods (e.g., crops, produce) that will be delivered at a future date. This is an exception to the general rule of asset-backed finance, permitted for agriculture.

How MMIC uses it: Agriculture finance — MMIC pre-finances the planting season by purchasing crops in advance at a fair price, supporting farmers before harvest.

Example: MMIC pays Hassan $500 upfront for sorghum to be delivered at harvest in 4 months. Hassan uses the cash for seeds and irrigation.
اِسْتِصْنَاع

Istisna'

Manufacturing Contract

What it means: A contract to manufacture or construct a specific asset according to agreed specifications, with deferred delivery. Payment can be made in stages, at delivery, or after delivery.

How MMIC uses it: Used for financing construction of small assets like boats for fishers, storage facilities for farmers, or custom-built agricultural infrastructure.

Example: MMIC finances the construction of a fishing boat for a cooperative, making stage payments to the boatbuilder over 3 months.
إِجَارَة

Ijarah

Leasing

What it means: MMIC purchases an asset and leases it to the client for an agreed rental period. The client pays rent, and at the end of the lease period, ownership may transfer to the client (Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek).

How MMIC uses it: Used for FursadMaal (motorcycles, equipment) and E-Fursad (home appliances, solar panels) where clients prefer a lease-to-own structure.

Example: MMIC leases a solar power system to a family for $30/month. After 24 months, the system transfers to the family at no extra cost.
قَرْض حَسَن

Qard Hasan

Benevolent Loan

What it means: A benevolent, interest-free loan given purely to help those in need. The borrower repays only the principal — no profit for the lender. This is considered one of the most virtuous acts in Islamic finance.

How MMIC uses it: Targeted at extremely vulnerable women, persons recovering from crisis, or first-time borrowers who need a starting push with minimal risk.

Example: MMIC provides a $100 Qard Hasan to a widow to stock her market stall. She repays $100 in small weekly amounts — nothing more.
